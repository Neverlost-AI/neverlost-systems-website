The patient-side coordination gap

Patients shouldn’t have to start their story over at every appointment.

Connecting records, providers, treatments, and follow-up requires time and knowledge that patients and families often have the least capacity to spare. That work competes with the energy and concentration they need to manage their symptoms.

Two perspectives. One appointment.

An illustration of the shared information need.

The patient is thinking

“I need you to understand two years of problems, symptoms, changes, and what I’ve already tried.”

The provider is thinking

“I need to understand two years of problems, symptoms, changes, and what’s already been done.”

Carry the larger story forward, prepare what matters for the appointment, and keep the deeper context available when it is needed.

Our guiding question

What work can we take off the patient’s plate?

We’re building Neverlost to reduce how much patients have to organize, remember, and reconstruct as they move through care.

About

Why I built Neverlost

I began with a question

What happened to me?

As my own health situation became more complicated, I spent years trying to understand it. Eventually, more people began holding different pieces of the answer.

The problem changed

What would it look like if the context carried forward?

Every new provider, system, application, insurance question, or major decision could require some version of the story to be reconstructed again.

The question became larger than my own case.

That process became the starting point for Neverlost.

I began documenting what helped move my care forward and turning those processes into workflows and software. The goal is to reduce how much someone has to prepare before support becomes useful.
